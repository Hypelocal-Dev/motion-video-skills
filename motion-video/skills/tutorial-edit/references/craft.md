# Craft: the calls that make a tutorial edit work

Make these calls by default. Change one only when the person asks or the recording
gives a reason.

## The open

- **Set the table.** As the narrator starts their intro ("In this tutorial I'll show
  you..."), bring up an agenda slide: the title on the left, the steps as a numbered
  list on the right. The steps appear one by one over 3-4 seconds.
- **Hold it for the whole intro sentence** (often about 10 seconds), and cut away on
  the next sentence, usually the start of the example. 3-5 seconds is too short to read
  six items, and cutting mid-sentence lands on a dull screen.
- The agenda is 4-7 steps, each 2-5 words, written as actions ("Write the brief").
- Use a cold open on the payoff only when the person asks for one.

## Structure

- **Chapters echo the agenda.** Each chapter card shows the same list with the current
  step highlighted and finished steps checked. Viewers always know where they are.
- A chapter card is a 2-second muted hold in the cut, placed right before the step's
  first sentence.
- End on the payoff (the result on screen), then the narrator's closing point, then
  the end card.

## Cutting

- Keep every step needed to reproduce the result. Cut retakes, filler, "let me just
  check", narration of waiting, and side tangents.
- **Never make people watch typing.** While the narrator types a prompt or a config,
  keep only the lines that explain it and show the content as a side panel that builds
  item by item.
- **Keep mistakes that teach.** A wrong setting, a wrong browser or a failed step stays,
  marked with a "Gotcha" chip and then a "Fix" chip. Cut mistakes that teach nothing.
- **Waiting becomes a fast-forward.** Play the tool working at 8-16x, muted, with a
  fast-forward badge that names the real time skipped ("2.7 min of the agent working").
  It stays honest about how long things take without dead air.
- Pauses inside speech get cut (the tighten settings); set `"tighten": false` on a
  segment where the pause is the point.
- Cut on the narrator's sentence boundaries. Check every edit point; a clipped last
  word is the most common error.

## Graphics

- **One graphic at a time.** Never stack a panel, a chip and a note at once.
- **Side panel** when the narrator lists or types something (rules, settings, findings).
  The screen slides left into a framed card, and the panel builds as each item is said.
- **Sketch diagram** (2-4 per video) for the ideas that make the method work: how the
  thing sorts or flows (`sort`), and a 3-step process (`steps`). These are the moments
  people screenshot.
- **Stat card** for a real number the tool produced (threads read, hours saved). Count
  it up.
- **Chips** for tips, gotchas, fixes and results. Short: label plus 3-8 words.
- **Notes** (handwriting) for the aside a good teacher would scribble: "folder =
  memory it keeps between runs".
- **Pull quote** for the one line that is the video's thesis. Highlight 1-2 words.
- **Kinetic close** on the narrator's closing point (2-3 short lines).
- **Punch-ins** on the UI the narrator points at, and a slow one on any stretch of 12
  seconds or more with no other motion. Ease in and out over 0.8 s.

## Captions

- Full captions, a few words at a time, in a dark pill at the bottom, with the spoken
  word highlighted. Feeds autoplay muted.
- Fix every product and brand name. Drop "um" and "uh". Hide captions under full-frame
  text (quote, kinetic close, end card), not under the agenda or chapters.

## Sound

- Level the voice to about -14 LUFS (the cut tool does this).
- A quiet bed under the voice, carved so it doesn't fight it; it lifts on chapter cards,
  fast-forwards and the end card.
- Small effects: whoosh on cards, pop on chips, tick on list items. Keep them quiet.

## Inspo and brand

- Inspo sets the vibe, caption type and kinds of motion. Never copy a video's signature
  look; translate it into the brand's colors and type so nobody can name the source.
- Brand colors and fonts drive every card. The logo appears on the agenda and the end
  card only.

## Privacy

- Sweep the whole cut before building graphics. Cover anything that isn't the product
  being demoed: desktops, Slack, email, password managers, other chats, other clients.
- A trim that saves a word can pull a private frame back in. Re-sweep after re-cuts.
